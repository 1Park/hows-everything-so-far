How's Everything So Far?

A serving robot notices your empty glass, asks whether you would like another, and acts on your answer — with every model running on the board.

What makes this board special?

It has an NPU.
It can run AI on the device itself.

So we went looking for a place where on-device is not a preference but a requirement.

Restaurant serving robots

The Wi-Fi is unreliable

Dining rooms are full of people, metal and microwaves. A robot that stops working when the signal drops is not a robot anyone keeps.

Faces and voices stay at the table

A robot that watches tables and listens to guests should not be sending them to someone else's servers. Nothing leaves the device.

So the robot has to think locally

That is exactly what the NPU on this board is for, and it is why the whole pipeline here runs on it.

The problem: empty glasses

Staff are busy.
Guests wait.
The restaurant loses refill sales.

It is a small thing to notice and an easy thing to miss, which is what makes it worth automating. HESF lets the robot see the empty glass and ask the guest whether they would like another drink — before anyone has to wave somebody down.

The flow — all of it on one board

  1. See

    YOLO finds the glasses, then DINOv2 reads each one: which drink, and how full. NPU · about 0.1s per frame

  2. Ask

    If a glass is low, the robot asks "Would you like another one?" with Piper. NPU · voice decoder as a .pte

  3. Listen

    The guest's answer is recorded on the board and turned into text by Whisper. CPU · 4 of 8 cores

  4. Understand

    Gemma 4 returns the intent — refill, no thanks, cancel, or didn't understand — and handles "not coke, coffee please". NPU · about 2s

  5. Act

    The microcontroller runs the refill sequence and shows its status. STM32H5 · one command over Arduino Bridge

How it fits together

One Linux process owns the camera. The microcontroller owns the pins. Between them is a single command, not a video feed.

Is it really the NPU?

The graph is delegated

Each .pte holds a QNN delegate and no CPU kernels — not one aten:: operator left in the program.

The DSP is in use

While inference runs, the vision and language processes hold /dev/fastrpc-cdsp handles: work is crossing to Hexagon.

What we do not claim

We exported no CPU baseline, so we quote no speed-up. Speech recognition runs on the CPU and is labelled that way everywhere.

It is not only about drinks

  • A hospital ward. The same loop watches an IV bag instead of a glass and calls a nurse the moment it runs low.
  • Privacy matters even more there. Patients and their rooms never leave the device, which is the whole reason this runs on the NPU.
  • The module rides, it does not drive. Navigation stays with the robot; this looks after the table it stopped beside.
  • The arm is the easy part to add. The microcontroller already runs a bounded sequence with a host watchdog — today it drives a display, not a gripper.
